    In simple words

    What is AQI — the big number?

    A score from 0 to 500 for how clean or dirty the air is. Lower is better: under 50 is clean, 100+ starts to bother sensitive people, 200+ is bad for everyone.

    What is PM2.5?

    The tiniest bits of smoke and dust floating in the air — far thinner than a hair. They go deep into your lungs, which is why they matter most for your health.

    Why do other apps (aqi.in, CPCB) show a different number?

    We use a global model that estimates the air over a wide area. Apps like aqi.in or CPCB use measuring machines on the ground at specific spots. Both are right — they just look from different places. Pollution changes street by street, so numbers rarely match exactly.

    What does “µg/m³” mean?

    It's just the unit for how much of a pollutant is in the air (micrograms per cubic metre). You can ignore it — just compare each number with the safe limit shown next to it.

    What's the photo in the background?

    When you search a place, the banner shows a real photo of a famous polluted area from the same country — so you can see what heavy air pollution actually looks like there. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons; hover the caption to see who took it.

    Can air pollution be fixed?

    Yes — and faster than most people think. London's Great Smog, Los Angeles' smog and Beijing's worst years all eased sharply once the sources were tackled: cleaner fuels, filters on chimneys, no open burning, and more buses, trains and cycles. The “What's going wrong — and how to fix it” card above lists what you can do today, and what your city, state or country can do next.

    Where does this data come from?
